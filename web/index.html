01 · How it's calculated

One line of math: weight the mid by who is waiting

Drag the resting size on each side. The micro-price slides along the spread — toward the heavier queue's opposite price, because a thick bid gets consumed slower and pushes the fair value up. Everything updates live.

μ = Pask · Vbid / (Vbid+Vask)  +  Pbid · Vask / (Vbid+Vask)
μ = 100.02 · 0.69 + 99.98 · 0.31 = 100.0076
Heavier bid → micro sits above the mid by +7.6 bp → upward pressure.

The beam tilts with imbalance; the marker below shows where the micro-price falls between the best bid and best ask. At a balanced book it rests exactly on the mid.

02 · The hypothesis

If the tilt is information, the mid should follow it

The mechanism above is just a definition — it proves nothing on its own. The testable claim: when the micro-price leans up at tick t, the mid-price should tend to rise over the next few ticks. So we measured it on real FI-2010 data. The result is real, and it is not a fairy tale.

03 · What the data actually says

Real FI-2010 — measured, not asserted

Signal = micro − mid at tick t. Outcome = sign of the mid-price change over the next k ticks. Hit-rate is the share of ticks where the sign matched. 50% is a coin flip.

Numbers computed live from 362,400 real FI-2010 ticks (data/fi2010.csv) — reproduce with scripts/compute_signal_stats.py.

What this is not

This is a feature study, not a trading result. Numbers are in-sample on a single instrument, on the normalized FI-2010 benchmark (which compresses the price grid and understates the raw edge). There is no fill model, no costs, no PnL. A 52–57% directional hit-rate is a real signal, not a strategy.
